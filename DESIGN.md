# Design

## Audience and intent (written before generating anything)

> DRAFT: confirm or edit these in your own words before the first user test.

- **Who watches:** my family and relatives, including younger cousins and relatives abroad who were children or not yet born at the Millennium. They watch on a phone, usually vertical, in a family group chat.
- **Where:** WhatsApp, Telegram, and a public post.
- **What they should feel at the end:** warmth and longing for a time when everyone was in one room laughing, and a wish to call a relative.
- **One sentence brief (define):** how might we help younger relatives feel what the Millennium night was like inside our family home?

## Story

Logline: a fictional family laughs, eats and counts down together on the night the Millennium began. Narrated in the first person plural by a grown child remembering ("our compound", "Mother", "Grandmother"). Ten shots, about 7 s each, with one idea and one movement per shot. The reason for each shot is stored in `storyboard.json` (`shots[].reason`).

Shot sizes vary on purpose: wide to place us (S01), mediums for what happens (S02, S03, S05), a close shot for the quiet beat (S06), a wide for the fireworks release (S08), and a close on Grandmother for the ending (S10).

Characters are fictional, in a hand-painted illustration style. This avoids real faces and reduces the identity-drift problem of photoreal faces.
